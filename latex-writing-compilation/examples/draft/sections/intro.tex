\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Graph neural networks trained on molecular graphs have become the default
surrogate for quantum-chemical labels \citep{zhang2022gnn}, and polymer
informatics has adopted them for dielectric and thermal properties
\citep{liu2021polymer}. Two difficulties recur. First, electrostatic
interactions decay slowly with distance, so a message-passing network that
truncates at $L$ hops silently drops the tail of the potential; our attention
term addresses this with a global, low-rank branch (Section~\ref{sec:method},
\eqref{eq:attention}). Second, reported gains are frequently measured under random
splits, which leak near-duplicate monomers; we therefore evaluate only under
scaffold splits \citep{wang2020scaffold} and report every hyper-parameter used
in Section~\ref{sec:exp}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A sparse-attention readout that keeps full electrostatic context at
$O(N\log N)$ cost. (ii) A scaffold-split protocol with five folds and fixed
seeds. (iii) A complete, compilable source tree: the PDF in this submission is
produced by the commands recorded in the reproduction notes, not by an
image-editing tool.
